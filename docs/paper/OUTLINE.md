# Paper outline

**Format:** ACL workshop short paper, 8 pages including references (4–6 pages body + refs).

**Target venue:** TrustNLP @ ACL 2026 + concurrent arXiv preprint.

The outline below is the skeleton. Each section names what goes in it, what's already in the codebase / docs to draw from, and what's still to be written.

---

## 1. Introduction (~0.5 page)

**The hook.** A short concrete example: a model writes *"the trial enrolled 1000 patients"* with a citation to a span that says *"the trial enrolled 240 patients."* The citation is well-formed. The link works. The claim is false. Every existing attribution metric calls this "cited with high relevance." That's the bug.

**The argument.** "Is this citation correct?" is treated as one signal but is actually three independent constraints. Most prior work conflates them, which hides the practitioner's diagnostic question: *which rule failed?*

**The contribution paragraph.** Three sentences:
1. We decompose citation correctness into existence, in-context, and support.
2. We build a 300-example FDA-drug-label benchmark with controlled failure injection.
3. We show the three rules catch non-overlapping classes of errors (target: ≥80% disjoint) and that the composed validator improves citation F1 by [Y] points over the strongest single-rule baseline.

**Roadmap sentence.** Section 2 places the work; §3 introduces the rules; §4 the benchmark; §5 the experiments; §6 limitations + future work.

**Draws from:** none yet. Write fresh.

---

## 2. Related work (~0.75 page)

**Tight. This is what kills most workshop submissions.** Cover:

- **Attribution and faithfulness lineage.** Rashkin '23 (AIS), Bohnet '22 (attributed QA), Honovich '22 (TRUE, NLI-for-faithfulness).
- **RAG citation benchmarks.** Gao '23 (ALCE), HAGRID, ExpertQA. Closest comparison points; explicit comparison table belongs in §5.
- **Span-level citing.** Menick '22 (GopherCite). Names the closest prior art on span-level claims, but their work cites with quotes generated by the model — not validated against retrieval.
- **Verifiability and post-hoc revision.** Liu '23 (verifiability in search engines), RARR (Gao '22), Self-RAG (Asai '24).
- **Frameworks.** RAGAS (Es '23) for rule-based RAG eval. We position the three-rule decomposition as the right granularity for RAGAS-style frameworks to adopt.
- **Contrast-set methodology.** Gardner '20. Defends the synthetic-failure approach.

**The gap paragraph.** After all that: "No prior work, to our knowledge, separates existence, in-context, and support as independent failure modes with separate measurements, and no prior work shows they catch distinct errors." This sentence is what we earn by reading the field.

**Draws from:** [`related-work-table.md`](related-work-table.md) once filled in.

## 3. The three rules (~1 page)

**Subsection 3.1: Existence.** What it is, when it fails, why it's necessary, how it's implemented (DB primary-key lookup). One paragraph.

**Subsection 3.2: In-context.** Same shape. The closed-loop constraint — the model can only cite spans it was *shown*. The HashSet check. One paragraph.

**Subsection 3.3: Support.** Same shape. The NLI cross-encoder over (span_text, sentence) pairs. Three-class output. One paragraph.

**Subsection 3.4: Composition + strict-wins.** How the three rules compose. The "strict-wins" aggregation (any contradicts wins, else any neutral wins, else supports). Why we ordered existence before in-context (each gate cleanly attributes a class).

**Subsection 3.5: Validator output.** The four refusal types: `Uncited`, `UnknownSpan`, `OutOfContext`, `Unsupported`, `Contradicted`. Implementation note: deployment philosophy is "refuse or resolve" — never silently degrade.

**Draws from:** [`crates/evidence-core/src/query/mod.rs`](../../crates/evidence-core/src/query/mod.rs), [`docs/EVAL.md`](../EVAL.md). Lift the prose; tighten for paper.

## 4. Benchmark (~1 page)

**Subsection 4.1: Corpus.** ~50 FDA drug labels from DailyMed. Token / page / span statistics table. Sample size justification (workshop short paper, focused claim, narrow domain).

**Subsection 4.2: Question types.** Five categories, ~60 examples each. Single-span, multi-span, negation, quantitative, contraindication / interaction. Worked example per category.

**Subsection 4.3: Manual verification.** Two-pass: LLM draft → manual verify. Author-only verification (acknowledged limitation). Verification protocol.

**Subsection 4.4: Failure injection.** For each verified example, generate three failed-citation variants via named operators (Gardner '20):

- **Existence:** rewrite the cited span ID to one that doesn't resolve in the DB. Programmatic; uses an offset above the parent's max span ID.
- **In-context:** add a duplicate-text span at a fresh ID, outside the prompt's chunk set, and rewrite the citation to point at it. Programmatic; the duplicate text controls for the relevance confound (the model cited *something* with the right text — just not from the right place).
- **Support:** apply an author-supplied sentence rewrite labeled `unsupported` (off-topic substitution) or `contradicted` (negation flip / number perturbation / entity swap). Each `valid` example carries 0–N support mutations in the dataset TOML.

The implementation ships as `evidence-eval inject` — a CLI subcommand that reads a dataset of valid seeds and writes the augmented contrast set. Operators are named, deterministic, and individually testable; the paper's appendix can list them in full without hand-waving.

The synthetic-failure approach is defensible because each failure is generated by a specific named operator. The natural-failure supplement (§4.5) defends against the "your injected failures are too easy" reviewer attack.

**Subsection 4.5: Natural-failure supplement.** Real LLM outputs (Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B) on the benchmark questions, no injection. Author + 1 co-author rate failures into our taxonomy. Smaller (~80 examples) but methodologically real.

**Draws from:** [`crates/evidence-eval/datasets/bootstrap.toml`](../../crates/evidence-eval/datasets/bootstrap.toml) (current 30-example shape) extended to 300. The TOML format is the artifact released alongside the paper.

## 5. Experiments (~1.5 pages)

**Subsection 5.1: Setup.** Models (3 LLM backends + DeBERTa-v3-large NLI), retrieval (BM25 + bge-small via RRF, with bge-reranker-base on top). Compute budget. Latency table.

**Subsection 5.2: Per-rule isolation.** Each rule applied alone. F1 on detecting its labeled failure class. **The diagonal in the catch matrix.** Reproduces the bootstrap result at scale.

**Subsection 5.3: Non-overlap.** The headline figure of the paper. Confusion matrix showing what rule 3 catches vs. rules 1+2 vs. rules 2+3, etc. Quantify disjointness. This is the empirical kernel.

**Subsection 5.4: Composition + ablation.** Cumulative F1 lift as each rule is added. Stop ablating when the lift drops below noise.

**Subsection 5.5: Comparison to ALCE.** ~50-example ALCE subset translated into our framework. Show that our composed validator scores comparably or better on ALCE's metrics, validating the decomposition isn't just a benchmark artifact.

**Subsection 5.6: Natural-failure run.** Real LLM hallucinations (no injection) on the benchmark. Per-rule catch rate. Human-eval agreement.

**Subsection 5.7: Cost analysis.** Latency per rule. Cumulative validator overhead vs. retrieval. (Industry readers care.)

**Draws from:** experiments to be run Weeks 6–8. Each subsection currently has placeholder numbers; replace with real measurements before publication.

## 6. Limitations (~0.5 page)

**Write this section first.** Forces honesty; prevents reviewer surprise.

- Benchmark domain is narrow (FDA labels). No generalization claim.
- Benchmark size is small (300 examples). Workshop-appropriate; not main-conf-strength.
- Manual verification is author-only for the synthetic corpus; co-author rates the natural-failure supplement.
- NLI cross-encoder errors propagate. We measure NLI accuracy independently as a separate column.
- Failure injection is by construction. The natural-failure supplement (§5.6) addresses this partially but does not eliminate the concern.
- We do not measure user trust, downstream decision quality, or workflow effects. That's future work.

**Draws from:** [`CLAIMS.md`](CLAIMS.md) "where we are likely to be attacked" section.

One paragraph summarizing the empirical claim. Three sentences on future work: (1) larger-scale benchmark across domains; (2) clause-level support decomposition (FActScore-style); (3) integration into production workflows with user studies.

Final sentence: pointer to the open-source artifact (`evidence`, link, license, version pinned to the paper's experiments).

## Artifact submission

ACL workshops increasingly accept an artifact appendix. We submit:
- The `evidence` repository, tagged at the version used for the paper's experiments.
- The 300-example benchmark + 900 failure-injected variants as a TOML file.
- The exact eval-harness command-line invocations that reproduce every table.
- Reproducibility checklist (model versions, hardware, random seeds).

## What's NOT in the paper

- The whole `evidence-cli` and Tauri desktop story. The paper doesn't need it. The artifact mention is enough.
- Marketing positioning ("auditable AI for high-stakes domains"). Keep the README's framing out of the academic paper.
- Krux AI commercial context. Acknowledge as author affiliation; do not pitch.
- "Closed-Loop Citation" as a top-level name. The empirical claim is the headline; the methodology name is, at most, a section subtitle.
